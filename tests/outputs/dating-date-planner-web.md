## The plan
**What:** Browse a bookshop together, then walk a few minutes to a specialty café for coffee.

**Where:** [Queen Books](https://www.blog.goodhood.ca/whats-good/queen-books-launches-independent-book-store-in-leslieville-toronto), 914 Queen St. E, Leslieville. It's an independent bookshop with a curated mix of fiction and non-fiction. Then [Pilot Coffee Roasters](https://www.blogto.com/cafes/tearo/), 983 Queen St. E, a short walk east. This is Pilot's original roastery café, formerly called Te Aro. It has indoor seating, a pastry counter and one of the biggest patios in the area.

**Other spots:**
- [Tango Palace Coffee Company](https://www.blogto.com/eat_drink/2021/07/tango-palace-coffee-toronto/), 1156 Queen St. E. It's been around since 1993 and is cozy, with antique chairs, stained-glass lamps and coffee and cake. It's a good second stop or a rainy-day pick. The listings I found are older, so check that it's still open.
- [Brickyard Grounds](https://torontolife.com/food/brickyard-grounds-toronto-cafes/), at Gerrard & Greenwood on the east edge of Leslieville. It's an indie café that serves Rufino beans and waffles. It's a bit further from Queen St., so use it as an alternative, not the main plan.
- Skip the Dundurn Press Bookshop. It's [closed Sundays](https://www.dundurn.com/about/the-dundurn-press-bookshop).

**When:** Sunday at 2:00 pm. Plan on 20–30 minutes in the bookshop and about an hour over coffee.

**Why it fits:** It combines her two favourite things, bookshops and good coffee. It's also close to her, and nothing on the plan depends on alcohol.

**Budget (my estimate, not prices I saw):** Two coffees and pastries should come to roughly $20–25, which leaves room for a paperback. One fun option is that you each pick a book for the other, and you buy hers. That keeps you around $50.

**Extend option:** If it's going well, walk east along Queen to Tango Palace for a slice of cake.
